# Learning roadmap: towards institutional-level trading skill

The goal isn't a bot that wins every trade. It's becoming someone who **makes good decisions under
uncertainty, sizes risk properly, tests ideas honestly, and can explain all of it clearly.**
That's what desks hire for, and it keeps options open: trading, risk, research, or energy and carbon markets.

Each stage ends with something concrete you can show.

---

## Stage 1: Foundations through this project (now → ~3 months)

**Learn**
- **Expectancy and R-multiples.** Why a 45% win rate can be profitable and a 90% win rate can go broke
- **Position sizing.** Risking a fixed % per trade, and why that keeps you alive
- **Reading a backtest.** Profit factor, drawdown, sample size, in-sample vs out-of-sample
- **Python basics and git.** Enough to read and run `orb_backtest.py` and change one setting yourself

**Do**
- Run H-001 to a written decision (`research/hypotheses.md`)
- Paper trade the bot on demo with a weekly review every weekend (`research/reviews/`)

**Resources (free)**
- *Python for Data Analysis*, Wes McKinney: free to read online at wesmckinney.com/book
- Khan Academy, Statistics and Probability: free

**Done when:** H-001 has a written verdict, and you have 8 weekly reviews with process grade A or B.

## Stage 2: Research skill (~3 → 9 months)

**Learn**
- How to reject ideas quickly and cheaply. Most ideas fail; speed of honest rejection is a skill
- Walk-forward testing, transaction-cost analysis, and why overfitting is the #1 failure in quant research
- How institutions actually trade: market structure, liquidity, who's on the other side of your trade
- Portfolio thinking: two uncorrelated strategies beat one great one

**Do**
- H-002 and H-003, for example a slower trend-following idea on daily data and a mean-reversion idea.
  Expect most to be rejected. That's normal.
- Write a **2–4 page research note** on what you tested and learned. This is your portfolio piece.

**Resources**
- *Quantitative Trading*, Ernest Chan: practical, aimed at independent traders
- *Trading and Exchanges*, Larry Harris: how markets and institutions really work
- *Fooled by Randomness*, Nassim Taleb: luck vs skill, written for traders

**Done when:** the research note exists and you could walk an interviewer through it in 10 minutes.

## Stage 3: Credentials and your edge (~6 → 18 months, overlaps Stage 2)

**Learn**
- **CFA Level I**: the broad, widely respected foundation (valuation, fixed income, derivatives, ethics).
  Alternatives: **FRM** if risk management appeals; **CMT** if technical trading does.
- **Energy and carbon markets.** This is where your day job is an advantage most traders don't have:
  how power is priced and traded (including regional pools like SAPP), carbon credits and compliance markets,
  and renewable energy certificates.

**Do**
- One market note connecting the two worlds, e.g. what drives carbon credit prices, or how a
  regional power market trades. Same research discipline as Stage 2.

**Done when:** CFA Level I passed (or booked), and the market note is written.

## Stage 4: Getting in (from the bottom up)

Realistic entry points, roughly easiest first:
- **Treasury or dealing desk at a local or regional bank** (FX, money markets). It's real trading and real risk limits
- **Risk or market-data analyst** at a bank, asset manager or commodity house
- **Energy or carbon trading firms**, or energy desks at banks, where your project-finance background stands out
- **Junior quant or research analyst roles**, where the GitHub repo and research note do the talking

**What you'll bring:** a clean GitHub repo, a written research process with honest rejections,
months of disciplined trade reviews, a credential, and real clean-energy finance experience.

---

## Habits to practise every day, starting now

1. **Plan before the trade.** Entry, stop, target and size are decided before you're in it.
2. **Size by risk, never by feel.** 1% means 1%.
3. **Accept the stop.** A stopped-out trade that followed the plan is a good trade.
4. **No revenge, no celebration.** A loss doesn't need making back today; a win doesn't earn extra size.
5. **Journal and review weekly.** Grade the process, not the P&L.
6. **Change the strategy only through a new hypothesis,** never mid-week and never after a bad day.
